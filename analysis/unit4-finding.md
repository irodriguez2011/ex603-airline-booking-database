**To:** Leadership Team
**Subject:** True Passenger No-Show Rate and the Flights to Investigate

**The true failure rate is 6%.** Of the 200 bookings on the platform, 12 were cancelled because the passenger did not show up. That is far below the 50% the dashboard reported. The wider group of all cancellations, which also covers passengers cancelling in advance, flights the airline cancelled, and failed payments, is 34 bookings, or 17%. Even that broader figure is a third of what the dashboard showed.

**The rate is not the same everywhere.** Across the 13 flights that have bookings, no-show rates range from 0% to almost 19%. Four flights had no no-shows at all. Comparing flights fairly takes care, because a flight with only a handful of bookings can look alarming after a single no-show. I therefore set a benchmark using only the eight flights with at least 15 bookings each. Together they handle 145 of the 200 bookings, and their combined no-show rate is 4.8%, slightly better than the platform overall. By region, Thailand (10% no-shows, and 11 of its 40 bookings cancelled) and Vietnam (7.9%) are above the platform rate, while Japan (2.3%) and Singapore (2.5%) are well below it.

**Flights I would investigate:**

1. **Flight VN202 (flight ID 2).** 3 no-shows in 16 bookings, or 18.8%, almost four times the benchmark. It has enough bookings that this is unlikely to be chance alone, and it is the first flight I would look into.
2. **Flight VN203 (flight ID 3).** 2 no-shows in 13 bookings (15.4%), plus 2 disputed bookings, so nearly a third of its bookings ended in a no-show or a dispute. It falls just short of the 15-booking benchmark group, so it needs a closer look rather than an immediate conclusion.
3. **Flight VN211 (flight ID 11).** Its no-show rate is normal (1 in 16), but it has the most cancellations of any flight: 5 of 16, or 31%, against a benchmark of 15%. It also has the lowest average fare ($11.94), because cancelled bookings are refunded to $0. This is a cancellation problem rather than a no-show problem, and it is costing revenue.

Flight VN204 (flight ID 4) also shows a high no-show rate (1 in 6), but with only six bookings that rate is not yet meaningful. I would watch it rather than act on it.

**A caution on these numbers.** The counts show how many no-shows each flight had, but not whether they came from different passengers or the same few people, or whether they cluster on particular dates. Before making any change to these flights, the next step is to review the individual no-show bookings for VN202 and VN203.
