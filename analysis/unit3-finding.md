**To:** Dashboard Reporting Team
**Subject:** Findings From Airline Booking Failure Rate Investigation

Our investigation found a significant difference between the failure rate reported by the dashboard and what the airline booking data actually shows. The dashboard reported that approximately 50% of bookings resulted in failures, which led leadership to freeze the quarterly route profitability payout. However, after reviewing all 200 bookings, we found that only 12 were recorded as passenger no-shows, representing a failure rate of 6%. Of the 200 bookings, 156 were confirmed, 10 were disputed, and 34 were cancelled, including the 12 recorded as passenger no-shows. These findings suggest that the dashboard's reported failure rate does not accurately represent the overall booking population.

Our investigation also identified a potential source of the discrepancy. When filtering bookings by cancellation reason, records with missing values can be unintentionally excluded from the results. In our testing, one filter returned only 22 bookings because it omitted 166 records with missing cancellation reasons. This demonstrates how a report can produce misleading conclusions when it does not account for the complete set of bookings. Although we have not confirmed that this exact issue caused the dashboard's original error, the findings highlight the importance of validating how booking failures are counted.

Based on these findings, I recommend that the dashboard team review and correct the reporting logic before leadership makes further decisions using the reported failure rate. The quarterly route profitability payout freeze should be reassessed because the available data does not support the reported 50% passenger no-show rate. Once the corrected figures and applicable payout criteria have been verified, leadership should determine whether the freeze is still justified.
